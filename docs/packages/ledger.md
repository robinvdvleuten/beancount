# `ledger`

Its exports are its interface: a name, method or field is exported only when another package uses it or an exported signature needs it. `decimal.Decimal` for amounts. Booking methods: `STRICT` (default), `STRICT_WITH_SIZE` (as `STRICT`, and when that cannot choose among several lots, the oldest lot of the reduction's size), `NONE`, `FIFO`, `LIFO`, `HIFO`, `AVERAGE` (like beancount, every reduction under it fails, and a merge cost `{*}`, which the parser reports, is booked like `{}`); the parser reports an open naming any other method and gives it the `booking_method` option in effect on its line. Like beancount's parser, Booking fixes up a compound cost inside total braces (`{{5 # 3 USD}}`, booked with its per-unit number ignored), a negative price and a total price without units before booking, which the parser reports, and books total braces without an amount (`{{}}`, `{{2020-01-01}}`) as the per-unit cost they amount to; `inventory.book` decides augment vs. reduce (beancount's `is_reduced_by`), reports which, and books a reduction at once, matched like beancount's against the lots at cost of either sign (STRICT weighs them all; the other methods book only those of the opposite sign), in its Currency group's scratch inventory, a copy made only for a reduction (`reducedBy` decides from per-sign lot counts, so a ledger that only adds lots stays linear); `inventory.augment` adds every other posting, with its cost per unit and dated, once interpolation has completed it and the transaction is booked; like beancount's `add_amount`, which stores the position anew, it gives a lot the inventory already holds (`100 USD` and `100.00 USD` on one date are one lot) the augmentation's cost number, which later reductions book at. The booker only groups, books each group's reductions against the inventories as they stood before the transaction (as beancount's `book_reductions` does, so one group never sees another's), drops a failed group, commits what the interpolation module returns, and then adds every booked posting to the inventories in posting order, a reduction as the lots it booked, so a lot keeps the cost of the posting that touched it last, like beancount's `add_position`. Interpolation (`ledger/interpolation.go`) completes one Currency group: given the group, the positions its reductions booked (a reduction weighs at the cost of each lot it booked, with the number the lot holds whatever its spec states, as beancount's `book_reductions` gives the posting the lot's cost) and the transaction's spec tolerances, `interpolate` returns each posting's completed amount, cost and price, whether it is left out, the amounts the amount-less posting is booked at, and the group's residuals, or the errors that make it a Dropped group (more than one missing number, decided once in `tooManyMissing`, or a missing number the residual does not determine); the booker expands the amount-less posting into one posting per group, since that spans groups. Booking publishes one record per booked posting of an Applied transaction, `Ledger.BookedPositions`: a `BookedPosition` per lot a reduction was booked against, otherwise one of its own, with signed units, a per-unit `BookedCost` (number at the precision Booking holds it, currency, lot date defaulting to the transaction's unless its units were interpolated, label) and `Reduced` (beancount's `Booking.REDUCED`); a Dropped group's postings have none. Apply replays the record, and Plugins, the query package and the printer read it rather than re-deriving lots or costs from the cost spec. `tolerances` (`ledger/tolerance.go`), built once per `Process` from the tolerance options and the only reader of them, answers every tolerance question: a transaction's tolerance per currency before booking (`spec`, which rounds interpolated numbers: the coarsest its numbers imply, or the finest under `use_precise_interpolation`, with a currency's default counting only when a posting names the currency, in its units, cost or a price that is not zero per unit) and once booked (`booked`, for the residual check, from the postings that leave no number to interpolation, as beancount's skips its `__automatic__` ones; the residual itself sums the booked weights in posting order, each sum rounded to 28 digits, as beancount's check does), and a balance assertion's (`balance`); the booker holds it, and `planPads` and the Balance handler ask it for an assertion's tolerance, to pad and to check (`checkBalance`) the assertion. `priceIndex` (`ledger/prices.go`) answers `GetPrice`: a pair's latest rate on or before the date, from its own prices or the inverses of the other direction's, never through a third currency; a zero price has no inverse. The ledger owns Valuation (`ledger/valuation.go`): `Position.AtCost`, `MarketValue`, `Convert` and `ConvertAmount` value a position on a date, and the query package's `cost()`, `value()` and `convert()` call them. `GetBalanceTree` (`ledger/balance_tree.go`) builds from the accounts, every one opened or posted to (like beancount, a posting to an account never opened is reported and still applied, so the account counts, with no open date), and the configured account-type names alone, stating each account's balance under a `Valuation` (At cost by default): it sums the `BookedPositions` of the account's postings in the period per lot and values them once, on the end date or else today. With `closed` it returns Closed balances, a balance sheet's as of a date: Equity gains Current earnings (the Income and Expenses positions through the date) and Current conversions (the negated cost balance of every posting) in the accounts `Config.CurrentAccounts()` names, summed in beancount's order so that 28-digit rounding gives its amounts every time (Current earnings account by account in account order, Current conversions per lot in the order the postings were applied, then each lot's cost), and, At market value or Converted to a currency, `Equity:Earnings:Unrealized`, whatever leaves the valued sheet off zero; a computed amount merges into a real account of its name, and one that comes out empty is left out. `Process` leaves the tree it is given as it was and returns the Processed tree, built from a copy of the directive list and of every transaction, note and document it writes to (and of any directive that repeats a metadata key: like beancount's parser, a transaction and a posting keep a key's first value and report each other one that is not the same Python object (`sameObject`), any other directive its last value in the key's first place, `ledger/metadata.go`); whatever the ledger keys by pointer (`BookedPositions`, a `Diagnostic`'s directive, balance differences) names its nodes, so every reader takes the tree `Process` returns. Its error is only a failure to process at all (a cancelled context, or directives that cannot be sorted); diagnostics, errors and warnings alike, come from `Diagnostics()` (`Errors()` for the errors), and `Config()` holds the options it processed under. Apply leaves booked postings in the Processed tree's `txn.Postings`, in beancount's booked order (by Currency group, sorted into groups in `ledger/groups.go` like beancount's `categorize_by_currency`, after which, as `replace_currencies` does, units and a price that leave out their currency take the ones categorizing resolved (only a posting of an account alone is an auto-posting: one with a cost or a price but no units, a total price Booking drops included, leaves out its units currency too) and a cost that leaves out its currency takes its group's, with or without its number, so a reduction at `{}` or `{"label"}` matches only the lots held in it; an amount-less posting becomes one posting per group with a residual); `BodyItems` keeps the source layout for the formatter. Like beancount's, a balance assertion sees the account's whole subtree (`Ledger.subtree`): `balance Assets:Bank` counts `Assets:Bank:Checking`, opened or not, the subtree's lots merged by key into one inventory as beancount adds every account's positions to one, a lot that sums to zero dropped, before their units are summed (60.00 USD here and -60.00 USD there leave 0, not 0.00). The pads module (`ledger/pad.go`) owns padding: like beancount's pad plugin, which runs after booking and before any assertion is checked, `planPads` reads the booked ledger once and decides every padding before validation starts. A pad is filled by the first assertion in a currency on its account or any account under it, measured against what the subtree holds and posted to the pad's account; each account with a pad is worked out on its own. The Pad handler's Apply applies its pad's paddings unbooked, their units as they are and without cost, so a padding takes effect at its pad and an assertion dated before the one that fills the pad sees it. A padding transaction is dated and positioned at its pad and carries its metadata; its postings carry the balance assertion's position and metadata, like beancount's.
